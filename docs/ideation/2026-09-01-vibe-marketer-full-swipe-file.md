## 2. Structure playbook (what to clone)

### Publishing system
- 2 sends/week steady (Tue/Wed + Thu/Fri), plus extra sends for live-session alerts and launches. Fixed send hour.
- Six formats rotate. Rough mix over 62 issues: roundup ~25%, essay ~20%, tutorial ~18%, recap ~13%, alert ~15%, promo/case ~9%.
- Everything is one funnel: session alert (before) → live session (free) → recap issue (after) → assets in paid community → occasionally a paid course. One event = 3+ issues of content.

### The fixed skeleton (non-promo issues)
1. Date + "Read online"
2. H1 = subject, then one-line dek
3. "Hey Vibe Marketer," (identity greeting, never "Hi everyone")
4. Hook: 1-3 lines. A quote, a question, or a contrarian claim. Always before any context.
5. Body with heavy tables (2-4 cols) instead of bullet runs
6. Per-item payoff: "The idea to steal:" / "Rule:" / "The takeaway:" / "Blocker: / momentum:"
7. "Wrapping up": zooms from the specifics to one principle, ends short
8. "Miscellaneous vibes": 7 curated links, lowercase, curiosity-gap phrasing
9. "Let's vibe": CTA ladder, 3-4 lines of "Wanna X? → product"
10. "Just hit reply. I read every message." + share-with-a-friend line
11. 3-option poll: Premium Vibes / Kinda Vibes / Not the vibe

### Copy mechanics observed everywhere
- Arrow chains as compression: "Find a bottleneck → Fix it → Measure → Find the next one".
- Numbers do the persuasion: $9k/day, 3,200 pages, $500 vs $5-10K, 15→800 pages, 30 min vs 30 days, 25k followers/45M impressions, $352.85 tokens.
- Before/after and old-way/new-way tables in nearly every essay.
- Named community members with links in every roundup; often a verbatim "Note from X:" quote. Readers become the content.
- Frameworks get names and acronyms relentlessly: V.I.B.E., CITED, AOA, SHIFT, 3 R's, Content DNA. A named framework = a reusable asset = a session = a course.
- Self-referential flywheel: their own businesses (Diesel Dudes, sports betting site, boringmarketing.com) supply the case studies; a reader copying the playbook becomes the next case study (Brandon).
- They dogfood publicly: the Jul 28 issue is literally "how we repurpose this newsletter", giving away the skill they use.
- Every recap gates the full asset (recording, deck, skill) inside the community. The newsletter gives real value; the community holds the artifacts.
- Sponsored/affiliate content is disclosed plainly ("Corey is compensated when someone buys it here").

### Subject line formulas (ranked by their usage)
1. Number + outcome: "$999 assessment into $8K retainers", "3,200 pages", "80 meetings"
2. Contrarian negation: "AI agents aren't enough", "You don't need a 24/7 agent", "You built a workflow, not a system"
3. "How I/How to" + specific: "How I built a virtual clone of myself..."
4. Direct question: "Are riches really in the niches?"
5. Accusation/diagnosis: "You've been using Claude Code wrong", "The simple step you are missing is costing you..."
6. Roundup count: "4 builds that...", "8 free resources", "12 ways"

## 3. Content strategy (what they write about, and the monetization arc)

Topic pillars across the year: (1) agent harnesses (OpenClaw → Hermes → their own Slack agent), (2) Claude Code for marketers, (3) skills/MCP as the unit of reusable expertise, (4) SEO/AEO/programmatic pages, (5) local "boring" businesses as AI playground, (6) one-person marketing team thesis, (7) audience building on X/LinkedIn, (8) paid ads with AI, (9) community member builds.

Monetization evolved in public: free sessions (Jan-Feb) → skills product + Skills Lab (Feb) → boringmarketing.com audit SaaS (Feb) → bundle $298 (Apr) → Slack agent $99/mo (Jun) → premium course $199 (Aug). The newsletter never sells cold; each product appears first as a story or a session.

Editorial thesis they repeat until it sticks: tools don't matter, systems do; context before agents; workflow < system; speed < momentum; document everything into skills; your data/expertise is the moat. One thesis, restated through different doors, roughly monthly.
