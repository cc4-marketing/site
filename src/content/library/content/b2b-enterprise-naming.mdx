Most naming approaches default to story and metaphor. That is the wrong register for a PMO platform, an internal tool, or a B2B campaign, where the name needs to read as business vocabulary on first pass, not a legend that needs explaining. This skill grew out of a real naming pass for an AI-powered PMO event: the first attempt used story-driven names, which scored well on memorability and badly on fit, since the audience was PMO leads at a B2B company, not a brand needing a myth to be remembered.

## What it does

The skill runs a naming brief through a structured process instead of freeform brainstorming.

**Two vocabulary lanes.** Plain category words the audience already uses daily (project, delivery, portfolio, the department name itself), and a short glossary of borrowed industry terms that carry built-in meaning without a story (Control Tower, Command Center, Single Pane of Glass, Nerve Center). It defaults to the plain lane and only reaches for a borrowed term when the plain version reads too flat.

**Core Name plus Subtitle.** Every candidate ships as a compact name (1 to 3 plain nouns) paired with a subtitle that states your existing pillars or value proposition directly, for example "Align, Predict, Optimize" or "Full Visibility, Total Control." It never invents new pillars, it reuses what your brief already defined.

**Two dials for revision.** A concreteness axis (myth, then borrowed industry term, then plain description) and a seniority axis (executive-abstract to operational-concrete). React in plain language, "too generic" or "too strange" or "the audience is more senior than that," and the skill moves one step along the right axis instead of starting over.

**Domain-word scope check.** If a candidate bakes in a department word like PMO, it produces both a keep-word variant and a broader swap-word variant, and names the tradeoff instead of choosing for you.

**Five-axis scoring matrix.** Finalists get scored 1 to 5 on short, B2B-aware, domain related, easy and not too broad, and not too distant or strange, presented as a table with a one-line verdict on the top pick and a backup.

# B2B Enterprise Naming

Turn a business naming brief (event, product, platform, campaign) into a shortlist grounded in real enterprise vocabulary, not myth or metaphor.

## Workflow

### Step 1: Brief intake
Capture what's being named, audience seniority (exec vs mid-level vs operational), and any pillars or tagline already agreed on. Reuse those verbatim later, never invent new ones. Note if a department word (PMO, Sales Ops, IT) is in play and whether the audience is exactly that function or broader.

### Step 2: Source vocabulary, two lanes
Lane A, plain category words the audience uses daily: project, delivery, portfolio, operations, the department name itself.
Lane B, borrowed industry terms that carry built-in meaning: Control Tower, Command Center, Single Pane of Glass, Nerve Center. Reach for Lane B only when Lane A reads too flat.

### Step 3: Structure candidates as Core Name: Subtitle
Core Name: 1 to 3 plain nouns. Subtitle: state the brief's existing pillars or value prop directly. Always generate both halves together.

### Step 4: Two dials for iteration
Concreteness axis: myth, then borrowed industry term, then plain descriptive. Seniority axis: executive-abstract to operational-concrete. On "too generic," move toward Lane B. On "too strange," move toward Lane A. On an audience-seniority correction, slide that axis and regenerate, reusing what already scored well.

### Step 5: Domain-word scope check
If a candidate bakes in a department word, produce a keep-word and a swap-word variant, and state the tradeoff.

### Step 6: Score with the B2B fit matrix
Score each finalist 1 to 5 on: short, B2B-aware, domain related, easy and not too broad, not too distant or strange. Present as a table, then a one-line verdict naming the top pick and one backup.

### Step 7: Deliver
End with the finalists written out in full as complete "Core Name: Subtitle" strings, plus one line on the recommendation.
